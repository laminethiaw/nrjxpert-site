🌐6.1 SGAM architecture (IEC SRD 63200)

The Smart Grid Architecture Model was born from the European mandate M/490 (CEN-CENELEC-ETSI group, today SEG-CG: documents SG-CG/M490/C, 2012 and SG-CG/M490/K, 2014) and is formalised internationally by IEC SRD 63200 (UML modelling, OWL ontology). It gives a common language to generators, transmission (TSO) and distribution (DSO) system operators, DER actors and consumers. Three uses: map a system on its layers, position the standards, delimit the scope of committees.

AxisComponentsRole
DomainsBulk generation, Transmission, Distribution, DER, Customer premisesEnergy value chain
ZonesProcess, Field, Station, Operation, Enterprise, MarketControl hierarchy, from primary equipment to the market
Layers (GWAC)Business, Function, Information, Communication, ComponentInteroperability: business, functions, semantics, transport, hardware
  • Business: business processes, regulatory framework, business models;
  • Function: use cases (IEC 62559) and requirements (IEC SRD 62913), technology-independent;
  • Information: semantics and syntax of the data (CIM, IEC 61850);
  • Communication: protocols, transport, addressing;
  • Component: primary equipment, IEDs, computers, grid-edge gateways.

In a systems engineering approach, the SGAM is used as a gap analysis tool: at each intersection (domain, zone, layer), one checks that a standard exists, that it is used by all actors and that no interoperability break remains.

🔸6.2 OT/IT operation: SCADA, ADMS, DERMS

The transition from passive distribution to active, DER-rich networks requires aligning operational technology (OT) and information technology (IT) (EPRI P174, IEEE 2030.11-2021 for DERMS):

SystemScopeHorizonNetwork modelEquivalent in NRJXpert
SCADATelecontrol, acquisition, elementary commandsHard real time (ms to s)Substations and IEDsPoints list (§ 6.8)
ADMSTopological optimisation, load flow, outages (OMS), qualitySeconds to minutesComplete model of the feedersDistribution load flow, FLISR, IVVC
DERMSAggregation, forecasting, collective control of DER, BESS and loadsMinutes to day-aheadAggregated or automation islandsTime series, OPF, VPP (network check)

The DMS guarantees physical security (thermal and voltage limits); the DERMS maximises the availability and yield of the DER. Two architectures: DMS-centric (single network model, but a strong real-time requirement for the ADMS) or by automation islands (the DERMS manages high-availability islands locally, the DMS keeps the consolidated view) — recommended when the existing ADMS is not real-time or the model is very large.

🔁6.3 Self-healing (FLISR / FDIR) and reliability

Evolution of automation: passive network → self-healing (FLISR) → Volt/VAR control (IVVC) → inverter control (IVVC/r). FLISR (Fault Location, Isolation and Service Restoration) combines remote-controlled switches, fault passage indicators (FPI) and an algorithm based on the network model:

  1. Detection: the nearest upstream protection (feeder breaker, recloser) trips; the FPIs send their alarms (IEC 60870-5-104). A transient fault is cleared by reclosing: momentary interruption only.
  2. Location: the fault is downstream of the last « lit » FPI and upstream of the FPIs that stayed « off ».
  3. Isolation: opening of the remote-controlled switches surrounding the located zone; a manual switch cannot be used, hence an isolated zone that is sometimes larger.
  4. Restoration: reclosing of the breaker for the upstream part, closing of a normally open (NO) switch to transfer the downstream part to a neighbouring feeder, after a load-flow check of voltage and currents; otherwise partial transfer by blocks.
  5. Then the crew locates the exact section, operates the manual switches and repairs.
Indices (IEEE 1366): SAIDI = Σ (customers interrupted × duration) / customers served (min/yr); SAIFI = Σ customers interrupted / customers served (/yr); MAIFI: momentary interruptions (≤ 3 min IEC, ≤ 5 min IEEE); ASAI = 1 − SAIDI/525 600; ENS = Σ P × duration (kWh).

In NRJXpert

Smart grid palette: switching device (feeder breaker, recloser, switch, tie switch; normal state closed / open; remote-controlled; FPI). An open switch is an open circuit for all calculations: the network meshed by the NO switches stays radial. In the dialog box of a load: number of customers. Calculation Self-healing (FLISR), reliability; settings in Simulation › DER study settings › Self-healing (element in fault, intervention times, minimum voltage and maximum loading after transfer, annual study, costs).

📉6.4 Integrated Volt/VAR control (IVVC, IVVC/r) and CVR

The voltage drop of a branch is ΔU/U ≈ (R·P + X·Q)/U². Control means: the on-load tap changer of the substation transformer (taps of ±0.625 %), capacitor steps, and — this is IVVC/r — the reactive power of the smart inverters (Volt/VAR and Volt/Watt functions, IEEE 1547-2018, EN 50549). Objectives: minimum losses, or CVR (Conservation Voltage Reduction): lowering the voltage within the contractual range reduces the consumption of voltage-sensitive loads (ZIP model).

ZIP load: P(V) = P0·(Z·v² + I·v + P); CVR factor ≈ 2Z + I (ΔP/P = CVRf × ΔV/V).

In NRJXpert: stepped capacitor bank symbol (number of steps, power of one step, initial state); PV inverters and batteries; calculation Volt/VAR control (IVVC), which compares over the load profile: S0 no control, S1 local control (Q(V) of the inverters, voltage relays of the capacitors), S2 centralised IVVC (tap + capacitors), S3 IVVC/r (+ reactive power of the inverters). Settings: « Volt/VAR » tab (objective losses / CVR, tap changer range, ZIP model).

If the substation transformer is a three-phase transformer with taps (“Tap changer (OLTC)” section of its dialog), IVVC uses its actual parameters: step, initial position, lowest and highest positions, and the ratio in the transformer's π model; it then proposes the position hour by hour and returns the tap positions (positive position = more primary turns = lower voltage). Without a tapped transformer, the tap changer stays the abstract device of the slack bus, set by the range of the “Volt/VAR” tab.

🏭6.5 Virtual power plants (VPP) and aggregation

The IEEE P2030.14 project defines the VPP: aggregation software that schedules a heterogeneous portfolio (PV, wind, BESS, back-up gensets, thermal storage, curtailable loads) as a single predictable and controllable asset, able to provide fast reserve, frequency response and demand response. IEEE 2030.4-2023 frames the control automation (SGIRM model). Orchestration couples very short-term forecasts and the predictive load flow of the DMS; if local Volt/VAR control is not enough, the DMS prepares preventive switching plans sent to the crews (crew mobile).

In NRJXpert, calculation Virtual power plant (VPP): economic dispatch (MILP) of the DER of the diagram with an exchange programme requested at the connection point (deviation penalty), the sale of fast reserve, demand response, EV charging; then network check by a load flow at each step (role of the DERMS) and flexibility envelope upwards and downwards. OpenADR demand response events: level 7.

🔋6.6 Microgrids, storage and smart inverters

A microgrid is a set of loads and DER within defined electrical boundaries, controllable as a single entity, in connected or islanded mode. IEEE 2030.7-2017: microgrid controller, transition functions (seamless islanding, resynchronisation) and dispatch. IEEE 2030.8-2018: tests and qualification (HIL benches; NRJXpert test bench: level 7). Inverter interconnection: IEEE 1547-2018, EN 50549-1/-2, IEC 62898 (microgrids).

🚗6.7 E-mobility: V1G, V2G and charging standards

Communication chain: vehicle —(ISO 15118-2/-20)→ charger (EVSE) —(OCPP 2.0.1)→ operator (CPO) / EMS —(OpenADR 2.0b, EEBUS)→ grid operator (DSO, ADMS, DERMS).

  • IEC 61851: hardware requirements, safety, charging modes 1 to 4 (AC and DC);
  • ISO 15118-20: bidirectional charging (V2G/V2X), wireless, Plug & Charge with PKI certificates;
  • OCPP 2.0.1: charger ↔ CPO, TLS security, payment, smart charging profiles (V1G, V2G);
  • OpenADR 2.0b: demand response, price and constraint signals between operators, aggregators and buildings or charging hubs.

In NRJXpert: charging station / fleet of electric vehicles symbol (number of vehicles, battery, charger power, arrival and departure times, states of charge, uncontrolled / V1G / V2G mode, coincidence factor). The load flow and the short circuit use the design power; time series use the uncontrolled charging profile; economic dispatch and the VPP optimise the charging.

📡6.8 Semantic data models and protocols

Two IEC pillars: the CIM on the IT side and IEC 61850 on the OT side, linked by a gateway (semantic mapping).

  • CIM (UML): IEC 61970 (transmission, EMS/SCADA, topology, power flow, state estimation), IEC 61968 (distribution: DMS, OMS, GIS, asset management, metering; profile 61968-13 CDPSM), IEC 62325 (markets).
  • IEC 61850: physical device → logical device (LD) → logical node (LN) → data object → attribute; e.g. XCBR (circuit breaker), XSWI (switch), MMXU (measurements); SCL language (IEC 61850-6, XML); MMS, GOOSE, SV services; extension to DER (IEC 61850-7-420) and to hydro plants (7-410).
  • AMI: DLMS/COSEM (IEC 62056; OBIS codes), G3-PLC (IEC 61334; CENELEC A band 35–91 kHz, OFDM, IEEE 802.15.4 / IPv6 / 6LoWPAN), cellular NB-IoT, LTE-M, 4G, 5G (calculation in level 7).
  • Telecontrol: IEC 60870-5-104 (TCP 2404, report by exception, time stamping, ASDU), DNP3 (IEEE 1815, classes 0 to 3), Modbus RTU/TCP (16-bit registers, no semantics).

🧱6.11 Modelling an architecture with the NRJXpert SGAM workshop

Section 6.1 introduced the SGAM framework; this section shows how to use it as a genuine modelling language to design, document and check the architecture of a smart grid project. The workshop opens with Tools › SGAM architecture. It works on the drawn network and produces deliverables that can go straight into a specification: SGAM planes, IEC 62559-2 use cases, traceability matrix, consistency findings, CIM model.

Objectives of the section

  • Describe a system with the elements and relations of the SGAM language, separating the logical viewpoint from the technical one.
  • Go from a drawn power network to a complete architecture on the five layers.
  • Write an IEC 62559-2 use case and derive the information exchanges and protocols from it.
  • Check cross-layer consistency, carry out a gap analysis and link the architecture to the CIM model of the network.

Duration: 8 h (lecture 2 h, labs 6 h). Prerequisites: sections 6.1, 6.2, 6.8.

6.11.1 The building blocks of the language

Each element belongs to a layer, has a kind, and sits in a cell (domain × zone). It also has a viewpoint: logical (what must be done, independently of technology: "islanding detection function") or technical (what it is done with: "vendor X interface relay, IEC 61850"). A good architecture starts from the logical view and allocates each logical element to one or more technical elements: this is what makes it possible to compare several bids on the same basis.

6.11.3 Six-step method

From the network map to the architecture file

  1. Draw or open the network (or import it with File › Import a CIM network model) and check it with a load flow.
  2. Generate the reference architecture, then review it layer by layer (Business … Component buttons).
  3. Add the project's use cases (Template use cases menu, or New in the Use cases tab) and complete their IEC 62559-2 sheet: scope, objectives, indicators, actors, scenario.
  4. Adjust: move misplaced elements (drag and drop), add logical elements and allocates relations, specify standards and protocols.
  5. Check (Check tab) and process each finding; read the traceability matrix: each goal must lead to a function, each function to components, information and protocols.
  6. Produce the deliverables: HTML report (planes, cube, use cases, findings), SGAM model as XML, planes as SVG, CIM network model (CGMES), telecontrol points list.

6.11.4 Consistency checks

SeverityCheckWhat to do
✖ errorrelation that breaks its rule (e.g. "communicates" between a function and a component; "realises" not going from a component to a function); relation to a deleted elementfix the direction or type of the relation
⚠ warningempty layer; function realised by no component; goal linked to no function; information object carried by no link; link without protocol or exchange without information objectcomplete the model: this is an architecture gap
⚠ warningprotocol used outside its usual zones (e.g. IEC 61850 towards the market, Modbus towards operation, OpenADR inside a substation)justify the choice or add a gateway: this is an interoperability gap
⚠ warninguse case without scenario or actor; step whose producer or receiver is not a model elementcomplete the sheet or create the missing element
ℹ informationfield (or station) to operation link without IEC 62351 or TLS; isolated component or actor; field device without standard; unallocated logical elementpoint to examine and take into the cyber-security analysis (Tools › Risk analysis, IEC 62443)

6.11.5 SGAM and CIM: two complementary models

The SGAM model describes who exchanges what, with which protocol; the CIM model describes the power network itself (equipment, connections, parameters, state). In the workshop, the information object "CIM network model" exchanged between the GIS and the ADMS (IEC 61968-100) is exactly the file produced by File › Export the network in CIM format: a CGMES archive with the EQ (equipment), TP (topology), SSH (steady-state hypothesis) and SV (load-flow results) profiles. Conversely, a CIM model received from a network operator can be imported, computed in NRJXpert, then used as the basis for generating the architecture.

6.11.7 Exercises

Exercise 1 — Placing elements in the SGAM

Give the layer, domain and zone of: (a) a 500 kW PV inverter; (b) its controller; (c) a DERMS; (d) a smart meter; (e) the MDMS; (f) a flexibility auction platform; (g) the OpenADR "EiEvent" message; (h) the G3-PLC network.

Show the solution

(a) Component, DER, Process; (b) Component, DER, Field; (c) Component, DER, Operation; (d) Component, Customer premises, Field; (e) Component, Customer premises (or Distribution), Enterprise; (f) Component, Transmission or Distribution, Market; (g) Information (object "Demand-response signals"), DER, Enterprise; (h) Communication, Customer premises, Field.

Exercise 2 — Choosing protocols

Propose a protocol for each link and justify it by the zone: IED ↔ IED in a substation; RTU → SCADA; inverter → DERMS; charger → CSMS; aggregator → industrial customer; HES → MDMS; meter → concentrator.

Show the solution

IEC 61850 GOOSE (Station, real time < 10 ms); IEC 60870-5-104 or DNP3 with IEC 62351 (Station → Operation); IEEE 2030.5 or IEC 61850-7-420, Modbus SunSpec locally (Field → Operation); OCPP 2.0.1 (Field → Operation); OpenADR 2.0b (Enterprise → Field); IEC 61968-9 / 61968-100 (Operation → Enterprise); DLMS/COSEM over G3-PLC (Field → Station).

Exercise 3 — Finding inconsistencies

A model contains: (1) "Aggregator" realises "Virtual power plant"; (2) "Meters" communicates "HES" without label; (3) "Improve continuity" with no relation; (4) "Modbus" between the SCADA (Operation) and the RTU (Station); (5) "Load-shedding function" (logical) allocates "Under-frequency relay" (technical). Which findings does the workshop produce?

Show the solution

(1) error: "realises" starts from a component, not an actor — use "participates"; (2) warning: protocol not specified; (3) warning: goal linked to no function; (4) warning: unusual protocol between Station and Operation (Modbus is a field protocol without time-stamping or security), plus information: station–operation link without IEC 62351; (5) correct.

Exercise 4 — Writing a use case

Write the IEC 62559-2 sheet for "Connecting a 1 MW PV generator to an MV feeder": scope, objectives, actors, 5-step scenario with the information exchanged, indicators.

Show the solution

Scope: connection request, study, connection and operation of a 1 MW PV plant. Objectives: connect without exceeding 1.05 pu or thermal limits, at least reinforcement cost. Actors: generator owner, DSO, engineering consultant, DERMS, inverter. Scenario: (1) the owner submits the request (power, connection point) to the DSO; (2) the DSO exports the feeder's CIM model to the consultant (CIM network model); (3) the consultant computes the hosting capacity with and without Q(V) and sends the report; (4) the DSO notifies the required settings (Q(V), P(f), FRT) to the owner; (5) in operation, the DERMS sends set points to the inverter (P/Q set points, IEEE 2030.5). Indicators: connection lead time, MW connectable without reinforcement, annual curtailment.

Exercise 5 — Traceability of a quantified goal

The goal is "reduce SAIDI by 40 %". Build the traceability chain down to the protocols and state which NRJXpert studies verify that the goal is met.

Show the solution

Goal (business) → FLISR function → components: ADMS, IEDs and remote-controlled switches, FPIs, RTU → information: measurements and states, switching orders, CIM network model → protocols: IEC 61850, IEC 60870-5-104 + IEC 62351, IEC 61968-100. Verification: annual reliability study ("Self-healing" calculation, annual option) with 2, 4 and 6 remote-controlled switches; keep the smallest number giving −40 % SAIDI with a positive discounted benefit (avoided ENS − cost of switches).

📋Level 6 quiz

1. The three axes of the SGAM are:

  1. voltage, frequency, power
  2. domains, zones, interoperability layers
  3. generation, transmission, distribution
  4. hardware, software, telecom
Domains (value chain), zones (control hierarchy), layers (Business to Component, from the GWAC).

2. A feeder protection IED is located, in the SGAM:

  1. Process zone
  2. Operation zone
  3. Field zone
  4. Enterprise zone
The breaker itself is in the Process zone; its IED in the Field zone; the SCADA in the Operation zone.

3. The DERMS differs from the ADMS by:

  1. aggregation and forecasting of DER, at intraday or day-ahead horizon
  2. millisecond telecontrol
  3. the absence of a network model
  4. wholesale market management
The ADMS guarantees short-term real-time physical security; the DERMS optimises the diversity of the DER.

4. In a FLISR sequence, a manual switch without FPI located in the faulted zone:

  1. is opened by the ADMS
  2. prevents any restoration
  3. trips on the fault
  4. enlarges the isolated zone up to the neighbouring remote-controlled switches
Example 6.3: manual INT-A2 → A2 and A3 isolated together until the crew arrives.

5. An 80 s interruption restored by FLISR counts in:

  1. the SAIDI
  2. the MAIFI
  3. the SAIFI
  4. the ASAI only
Below the momentary interruption threshold (3 min IEC, 5 min IEEE 1366), it is counted in the MAIFI.

6. IVVC/r adds to classical IVVC:

  1. the on-load tap changer
  2. capacitor steps
  3. control of the reactive power of smart inverters
  4. load shedding
It increases the hosting capacity without violating the voltage range or the thermal limits.

7. With loads 40 % Z, 30 % I, 30 % P, the CVR factor is about:

  1. 1.1
  2. 0.3
  3. 2
  4. 0
CVRf ≈ 2Z + I = 0.8 + 0.3 = 1.1: a 3 % voltage reduction → about 3.3 % less consumption.

8. To reconnect a 2 MVA island to the grid, IEEE 1547 requires:

  1. Δf ≤ 0.3 Hz, ΔV ≤ 10 %, Δφ ≤ 20°
  2. no condition
  3. Δf ≤ 1 Hz
  4. Δf ≤ 0.1 Hz, ΔV ≤ 3 %, Δφ ≤ 10°
Above 1.5 MVA, the tightest criteria of table 39 apply.

9. Which protocol links a charging station to the operator's system (CPO)?

  1. ISO 15118
  2. OCPP
  3. DLMS/COSEM
  4. IEC 60870-5-104
ISO 15118 links the vehicle to the charger; OCPP the charger to the CPO; OpenADR the CPO to the grid operator.

10. In IEC 61850, the measurement of voltages, currents and powers is carried by the logical node:

  1. XCBR
  2. CSWI
  3. MMXU
  4. PTOC
XCBR: circuit breaker; CSWI: switch controller; PTOC: overcurrent protection.

11. Modbus differs from the CIM and IEC 61850 by:

  1. the absence of a semantic model (16-bit registers)
  2. mandatory use of TLS
  3. a UML model
  4. SCL files
Hence the SunSpec models to give meaning to the inverter registers.

12. The owner's engineering phase in which the IEC 62559 use case sheets are written is:

  1. scoping
  2. bid analysis
  3. acceptance
  4. requirements engineering
Phase 2, with the SGAM mapping of the information nodes and interoperability needs.

13. In the SGAM workshop, a dashed frame denotes:

  1. a faulty component
  2. a component recalled as a watermark
  3. a logical element (what must be done)
  4. an element imported from a CIM file
Solid frame: technical element; dashed: logical element, to be allocated to one or more technical solutions.

14. The "realises" relation connects:

  1. a component to a function
  2. an actor to a function
  3. a goal to a function
  4. two components
Actor → function: "participates"; goal → function: "traces"; component → component: "communicates" or "exchanges".

15. An IEC 61850 MMS link between the SCADA (Operation) and a market platform (Market) triggers:

  1. no finding
  2. a relation error
  3. an empty layer
  4. an unusual-protocol-between-zones warning
IEC 61850 is designed for the Process to Operation zones; towards the market, the CIM messages of IEC 62325 are used.

16. The CGMES profile that contains the computed bus voltages is:

  1. EQ
  2. SV
  3. SSH
  4. TP
EQ: equipment; TP: topology; SSH: steady-state hypothesis (powers, states); SV: state variables (results).

17. In an IEC 62559-2 scenario, the workshop reports that a producer "is not an element of the model". You must:

  1. delete the step
  2. change layer
  3. use exactly the name of an actor or component of the model, or create it
  4. add a protocol
The scenario is linked to components, information and protocols only through element names.
