📘1. Setting up the room

  • Client: Java 17 or later and JavaFX 21 (mvn clean javafx:run, or a packaged version).
  • Calculation server: hosted remotely (VPS); nothing to install on the computers. Enter on each computer (or give to the students) the institution's access key: Simulation › Calculation and server settings, « Encrypted connection » ticked. One key per institution, valid for the academic year; report to the supplier any key circulated outside the institution (it will be revoked and replaced). Before the session, check that the room has Internet access and that the institution's firewall lets the server port through (Test the connection from one computer: it must show « valid key »). A whole class calculating at the same time is not a problem: each calculation weighs a few kilobytes and takes less than one second (a few seconds for the DER studies of level 5, up to about fifteen seconds for the test bench of level 7).
  • Unstable connection: schedule the network sessions (levels 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7) at times when the connection is reliable; the photovoltaic, wind and economics sessions (levels 3 and 4) work offline. The reference results of the tutorial allow marking even without the server.
  • Examples: when the tutorial is first opened, they are copied to ~/.nrjxpert/aide/tutoriel/exemples/; a student who modifies them keeps his or her version.
  • Weather data: downloading them also requires the Internet; prepare the CSV files in advance for an offline session.
  • Language: with the interface in English (Options › Language), the Help menu opens the English pages of the tutorial.

📐5. Exam subjects (2 h, documents allowed: formula sheet of the appendices)

Subject A — Electrification of a health post (technician)

  1. Draw up the energy balance of a health post (list of appliances provided).
  2. Size the array, battery, charge controller and inverter at 24 V for a worst month of 4.8 kWh/m²/d.
  3. Choose the cross-section of the array → controller cable (12 m, ΔU ≤ 3 %) and its protection.
  4. Calculate the annuity and the cost of the kWh over 15 years at 8 %.

Solution: follow example 3.3 and example 3.4; marks 5 + 7 + 4 + 4.

Subject B — Distribution network (engineer)

  1. Three-node network (data of lab 3): write Ybus in pu (base 10 MVA, 30 kV); do one Gauss-Seidel iteration.
  2. Comment on the NRJXpert results provided (Newton-Raphson): losses, reactive power of the PV node.
  3. Three-phase and phase-to-earth short circuit at node 3 (data provided), choice of the breaking capacity.
  4. A 2 MW PV plant is added at node 3: expected effects on the voltage and the losses; control law to propose.

Solution: examples 2.3, 2.4; lab 3; § 5.2. Marks 6 + 4 + 6 + 4.

Subject C — Hybrid project (master)

Compare for a 100 kW peak site: genset alone, PV + batteries, optimised hybrid (level 5 results provided). Discuss the discount rate, the price of diesel and the renewable share; conclude with a quantified recommendation.

  • Stability of an island microgrid (Saloum islands) with grid-forming batteries.
  • Update of the equipment database with market prices and sensitivity study of the LCOE.
  • Measurement / simulation comparison on an existing PV installation of the school.
  • Loss audit of a real feeder with smart-meter data (level 7) and AMI business case.

👩‍🏫7. Teaching tips

  • Require the hand calculation before using the software; mark the explanation of the differences more than the value obtained.
  • Vary one input at a time and ask for a qualitative prediction before running the calculation again.
  • Use local data (site weather, supplier prices, measured consumption profiles): motivation and the quality of the discussions improve markedly.
  • Recall the limits of the models: transformer ratio not represented by the server, indicative prices, typical days.
  • Unit entry errors (mH/H, kV/V, kW/W) are the first cause of absurd results: have the orders of magnitude checked.
